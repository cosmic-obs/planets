Planet Calendar · Dublin

Each night is one row, earliest at the top: a calendar year (the arrows beside it pick which, 1900 to 2050), or the six months either side of tonight. Each planet has its own colour and three lines through the nights.

  • Night
  • Astronomical twilight
  • Nautical twilight
  • Civil twilight
  • Daylight
  • Solid line: when the planet is highest in the sky
  • Line of up chevrons: when it rises (each line is named on the chart)
  • Line of down chevrons: when it sets
  • Its best nights: opposition, or for Mercury and Venus the evenings or mornings when it stands farthest from the Sun
  • Picked planet: shaded while it's up, solid where it's at least 10° up with the sky dark enough to see it
  • Picked planet: 10° up, the line for viewing; fainter dashed lines at 20° and 30°
  • Moonlight, with the Moon chip on: stronger as the Moon is fuller and higher
  • The night of the full Moon
  • Summer time: the times up the hour lines turn lilac and show the clock, an hour ahead of the ruler

The chart is drawn in the place's standard time, so the lines stay smooth through the year; the bar beside the dates marks summer time, when clocks run ahead of it. Times written out (the night tag, the planet's card, the readout under the pointer) are clock times, summer time included. Lines are drawn between sunset and sunrise only. Dark enough to see a planet means the Sun at least 6° down, or full darkness for Uranus and Neptune. Tap a date on the left to pick a night (tonight is picked when the page opens). Tap a line or its name to pick that planet. Its card shows where it is at the moment under the pointer, or wherever you last tapped. Pinch or scroll to zoom, drag to move. Tapping a planet's chip in the bar steps it from drawn to picked to off, and back to drawn. Sky turns the picked night into the sky overhead: each planet flies from its place on the night's row to where it stands in the sky (tapping the date tag or the night's label does the same).
